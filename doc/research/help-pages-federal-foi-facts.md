## 1. Acknowledgement (14 days) versus the decision period (30 days)

The Act sets two separate duties with two separate clocks.

### Acknowledgement

- Section 15(5)(a) requires the agency or Minister, "as soon as practicable but in any case not later than 14 days after the day on which the request is received", to "take all reasonable steps to enable the applicant to be notified that the request has been received".
- The duty is to take reasonable steps to enable notification, not to deliver a notice.
- Guidelines Part 3 v1.10, paragraph 3.244 (page 50), restates this.
  - It says the requirement "will be met by sending a notice of receipt to the contact address provided by the applicant".
  - It says the 14 days start "on the day after the request is received".
- The OAIC publishes a sample acknowledgement notice.
  Part 3 footnote 168 links to it. I did not open the notice itself.
- Part 3 v1.10 paragraph 3.268 and 3.269 (pages 55 and 56) treat the acknowledgement letter as a place to tell applicants about IC review rights.
  They describe this as "good practice", not as a legal requirement.

### Decision

- Section 15(5)(b) requires the agency or Minister, "not later than the end of the period of 30 days after the day on which the request is received", to "take all reasonable steps to enable the applicant to be notified of a decision on the request".
- Guidelines Part 3 v1.10, paragraph 3.245 (page 50), restates this.
  - The 30 days start on the day after the request is taken to be received.
  - The request must meet the formal requirements in s 15(2) and (2A).
- Paragraph 3.247 (page 50) says the period is calendar days, including public holidays and agency shutdown periods.
  The page I read cuts off mid-sentence, so I have not summarised the rule for a last day that falls on a weekend or public holiday.

### What can lengthen the 30 days

- Section 15(6): a further 30 days, if the agency determines in writing that consultation under s 26A, 27 or 27A makes this appropriate, and tells the applicant as soon as practicable.
- Section 15(7) and (8): a further 30 days to consult a foreign government, its authority or an international organisation.
- Section 15AA: an extension of no more than 30 days, if the applicant agrees in writing and the agency notifies the Information Commissioner as soon as practicable.
- Section 15AB: the Information Commissioner may extend by 30 days, or another period, on application by the agency for a complex or voluminous request.
- Section 31(2): the processing period does not run while a charge is unpaid, between the charge notice day and the day the charge is paid, changed or dropped.
- Guidelines Part 3 v1.10, paragraphs 3.255 to 3.258 (page 54), cover s 15AA.
  - The applicant's agreement must be written.
  - It must be sought before the processing period has expired.
  - Combined agreed extensions cannot exceed 30 days.

### Bearing on #708

- The requester in #708 said the 2018 Guidelines state the 14 day duty at s 15(5)(a).
  The current Act and the current Guidelines still contain that duty, in the wording above.
- I did not fetch the May 2018 Guidelines, so I have not checked the exact quotation in the #708 email against that version.
- The Act describes the acknowledgement as a separate duty from the decision, and the two periods are different (14 days and 30 days).

## 2. Internal review after a deemed refusal, and what s 15AC means for an applicant

### Deemed refusal (s 15AC)

- Section 15AC(1) applies when the initial decision period has ended and the applicant has not received notice of a decision.
- Under s 15AC(2) the initial decision period is the 30 days in s 15(5)(b), or that period as extended otherwise than under s 15AC.
- Under s 15AC(3), on the last day of that period, the principal officer of the agency or the Minister "is taken to have made a decision personally refusing to give access to the document".
- Under s 15AC(3)(b), notice of that decision is taken to have been given under s 26 on the same day.
- Section 53A carries a note to the same effect: a request not decided in time is taken to be refused (see s 15AC).
- Guidelines Part 3 v1.10, paragraphs 3.265 and 3.266 (page 55), restate this.

### Who can apply under s 15AC

- Section 15AC(4) says "the agency or Minister concerned may apply, in writing, to the Information Commissioner for further time".
- Section 15AC(5) lets the Information Commissioner allow further time, and s 15AC(6) lets the Commissioner attach conditions.
- Nothing in s 15AC gives the applicant a power to apply, or to ask the Commissioner to act.
- Guidelines Part 3 v1.10, paragraphs 3.272 to 3.274 (page 56), describe the application as made by "the decision-maker".
  Paragraph 3.274 says there is "no obligation" on the Commissioner to seek the applicant's views, though the Commissioner is not precluded from doing so.
- If further time is allowed and the agency decides within it and meets any conditions, s 15AC(7) says the deemed refusal "does not apply, and is taken never to have applied".
- If it does not, s 15AC(8) says the deemed refusal applies as if the initial period were extended by the time allowed.
- Section 15AC(9) says the Commissioner cannot allow further time a second time for that deemed decision.

### Internal review is not available for a deemed refusal

Four provisions point the same way.

- Section 54(1) applies to an access refusal decision "other than a decision made personally by the principal officer of the agency or the responsible Minister".
  Section 15AC(3)(a) deems the deemed refusal to be exactly such a decision.
- Section 54E(b) says Part VI does not apply to "a decision in relation to the provision of access to a document upon a request that is taken to have been made under section 15AC or 51DA".
- Section 52 (the guide to Part VI) says Part VI covers decisions "other than decisions made personally by the principal officer of an agency or the responsible Minister".
- Section 54D is a separate mechanism: a deemed affirmation after an internal review application is not decided within 30 days.
  It does not apply to the first-instance deemed refusal.

The Guidelines say the same in three places.

- Part 3 v1.10, paragraph 3.267 (page 55): "It is not open to an agency or minister to conduct an internal review of a deemed decision."
- Part 9 v1.5, paragraph 9.9 (page 5 of Part 9, PDF page 255 of the July 2026 compilation): an internal review is not available if the original decision was made personally by the principal officer, and "This includes a deemed access refusal decision".
  It adds that a person dissatisfied "will need to apply for IC review".
- Part 9 v1.5, paragraph 9.18 (page 7 of Part 9, PDF page 257): internal review is not available where "a decision is not made within the statutory timeframe and consequently a decision is deemed to have been made refusing access to a document under s 15AC" (citing s 54E(b)).

Part 3 v1.10, paragraph 3.269 (page 56) adds that if an applicant applies for internal review of a deemed refusal, agencies "should advise them of the IC review process".
It adds that agencies should tell applicants they "will continue to process the request and provide a statement of reasons".

### What s 15AC means for an applicant

- The applicant may apply for IC review of the deemed refusal.
  - Section 54L(1) and (2)(a) allow IC review of an access refusal decision.
  - Note 1 to s 54L says this may be made "regardless of whether the decision was the subject of internal review".
  - Guidelines Part 3 v1.10, paragraph 3.267, states the consequence of a deemed refusal is that the applicant "may apply for IC review (s 54L(2)(a))".
  - Guidelines Part 10 v1.12, paragraphs 10.5 and 10.6 (page 5 of Part 10, PDF page 269), say the same.
- Section 54S(1) sets the time limit for IC review of an access refusal decision at 60 days "after the day notice of the IC reviewable decision was given under section 26".
  Section 15AC(3)(b) deems that notice to be given on the last day of the initial decision period.
  Reading the two together, the 60 days run from that day.
  This is my reading of the two sections. I did not find a Guideline paragraph that states the 60 day count for a deemed refusal.
- Section 54T lets the Commissioner extend the time to apply for IC review, including after the 60 days have ended, if satisfied it is reasonable in all the circumstances.
- The agency can still decide the request after a deemed refusal.
  Part 3 v1.10, paragraph 3.270 (page 56): the agency "is still able to process the request and provide a statement of reasons".
  If IC review has been sought, "the decision under review by the Information Commissioner will be the deemed access refusal decision", and the statement of reasons "will be taken as submissions in the IC review".
  Section 54Y(2) covers the case where an actual decision is made under s 15AC(7) after an IC review application: the Commissioner treats the application as one for review of the actual decision.
- A s 15AA extension cannot be agreed once a request has become a deemed refusal.
  Part 3 v1.10, paragraph 3.258 (page 54).
- Charges: Part 3 v1.10, paragraph 3.267, says that once there is a deemed refusal, "the agency or minister cannot impose a charge for access (see Part 4 of these Guidelines)".
  I read this statement in the Guidelines. I did not trace it to a specific section of the Act or the Regulations, so I cannot say which provision it rests on.

### Bearing on #854

#854 proposes help copy saying that internal review is unavailable after a missed deadline, and that an applicant can ask the agency to make a "section 15AC" application to the OAIC to reinstate internal review.

- The first part matches the sources above (ss 54(1), 54E(b), Guidelines 3.267, 9.9, 9.18).
- On the second part:
  - Section 15AC(4) lets only the agency or Minister apply to the Commissioner. An applicant cannot make the application.
  - The Act does not use the word "reinstate" for internal review.
  - Section 15AC(7) says the deemed refusal is "taken never to have applied" if the agency decides within the further time and complies with conditions.
    I found no section or Guideline paragraph stating what that does to internal review rights over the later actual decision.
    I have not verified the proposition that applying under s 15AC "reinstates" a right to internal review.
- #854 also says an applicant may prefer to let time run out because "charge can no longer be imposed, even after timeframe is reinstated by the OAIC".
  The Guideline sentence in 3.267 (above) says no charge can be imposed once there is a deemed refusal.
  I found nothing in the sources I read that says whether that continues after the Commissioner allows further time under s 15AC and the deemed refusal is disapplied by s 15AC(7).
  This is unverified.
- On extensions: s 15AA requires the applicant's written agreement and a request to the applicant must come before the processing period has expired (Part 3 v1.10, paragraph 3.256).
  Whether agreeing is "preferable" is advice, and the sources do not say.

## 3. The s 93A Guidelines, and the content #840 describes

### What s 93A and s 15(5A) say now

- Section 93A(1): the Information Commissioner may issue guidelines for the purposes of the Act by written instrument.
- Section 93A(2): "regard must be had to any guidelines issued by the Information Commissioner under this section" when performing a function or exercising a power under the Act.
  Subsection (2)(c) names s 15(5A) (decisions on requests).
- Section 93A(3): guidelines "are not legislative instruments".
- Section 15(5A): in making a decision on a request, the agency or Minister must have regard to the guidelines issued for s 93A.
- The OAIC index page (updated 28 July 2026) says the Commissioner "can issue guidelines" under s 93A and that they must be had regard to.
  The Guidelines are a numbered set of Parts, with Part 8 marked "Superseded, content moved to Part 3".
- The current Parts relevant here are Part 3 v1.10 (February 2026), Part 9 v1.5 (April 2024) and Part 10 v1.12 (February 2026), all inside the July 2026 compilation.

### Part 3.48

- #840 says "Part 3.48 Guideline does not exist anymore" and quotes a statement that the Guidelines provide a request "can be posted on a public website and forwarded to a specified electronic address of the agency or the Minister", with a footnote naming Right to Know.
- Paragraph 3.48 of Part 3 v1.10 still exists.
  It begins at the bottom of page 12 and concerns the reasonable steps duty in s 24AB(4) (revising a request when a practical refusal reason exists).
  It does not contain the text #840 quotes.
- I searched the text of Part 3 v1.10 and Part 3 v1.8 (December 2021) for "public website" and found no match in either.
  I also read the formal requirements section in each.
- The quotation in #840 is attributed to an OAIC decision on the ATO matter (Fairless v Australian Taxation Office).
  I did not find or read that decision, and I did not fetch the 2016 or 2017 Guidelines that the decision would have cited.
  I cannot confirm what an earlier version of paragraph 3.48 said, or when it changed.
  That part of #840 is unverified.

### Where Right to Know appears in the current Part 3

- Part 3 v1.10, paragraph 3.33 (page 10), lists the formal requirements for a request.
  Footnote 22 names Right to Know (www.righttoknow.org.au) as an example.
- Footnote 22 says that the address given by the applicant "only needs to be sufficient to permit the agency to send notices under the FOI Act".
  It says that "Because the Right to Know website allows notices to be sent to the platform, use of that website satisfies s 15(2)(c)."
- Footnote 22 also says Right to Know "does not allow people to make FOI requests for their own, or some else's, personal information", and "encourages FOI officers to contact them to discuss any request that may result in disclosure of personal information".
- Part 3 v1.8 (December 2021) has a shorter footnote 20 naming the site, at page 13.
  It describes the website as automating the sending of requests and publishing all correspondence.
  It does not contain the "satisfies s 15(2)(c)" sentence.
- The same list, at paragraph 3.33 (continued on page 11), says a request may be sent by "electronic communication to an email or fax address specified by the agency or minister (s 15(2A))".
  Section 15(2A)(c) of the Act says the same: "sending by electronic communication to an electronic address specified by the agency or Minister".
- Section 15(2)(c) requires the request to "give details of how notices under this Act may be sent to the applicant (for example, by providing an electronic address to which notices may be sent by electronic communication)".

### Section 88

- #840 says "S88 does not exist anymore".
- Section 88 exists in the current Act, headed "Information Commissioner investigations - what are the investigation recommendations?".
  It defines "investigation recommendations" as the formal recommendations to the respondent agency that the Commissioner believes ought to be implemented.
- #840 quotes a recommendation that the ATO "process valid FOI requests made through the RTK website" under s 88.
  I did not read the original decision, so I cannot say which version of s 88 it relied on, or whether the old s 88 had different content.
  That is unverified.
